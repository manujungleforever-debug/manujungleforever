# Sustainable Tourism in the Manu Biosphere Reserve

The Manu Biosphere Reserve is one of the most important conservation areas in the world. But conservation doesn't happen in a vacuum — it requires active human stewardship. This is where sustainable tourism plays a crucial role.

## The Challenge of Conservation on Private Lands

Within the buffer zone of the Manu Biosphere Reserve, much of the land is privately owned by local farming families who have lived here for generations. These families face a fundamental economic choice: use their land for traditional agriculture (which often means clearing forest for crops or cattle), or find alternative livelihoods that allow the forest to stand.

Sustainable tourism offers a third path: using the land as it is — pristine and wild — to attract visitors who pay to experience its extraordinary biodiversity.

## How Hidden Jungle Cusco Supports Conservation

Our lodge and trails in Nuevo Eden are located on land that has been in the Araña family for three generations. Rather than clearing this land for agriculture, the family has chosen to protect it as a private ecological reserve — accessible only to our guided tour groups.

This means:

- **400 hectares of primary rainforest** protected from logging and agriculture
- **12 local families** employed directly by Hidden Jungle Cusco
- **Wildlife corridors** maintained between the lodge property and the national park
- **Environmental monitoring** — our guides document wildlife sightings that contribute to scientific databases

## The Ripple Effect

When you book a tour with Hidden Jungle Cusco, your money doesn't go to a large corporation or a foreign-owned resort chain. It stays in the community. It pays the salary of your local guide, buys food from local farmers, and funds the maintenance of trails that double as wildlife corridors.

This is the promise of sustainable tourism: that an experience created for visitors can simultaneously be the best possible outcome for the natural world.
