# Everything You Need to Know Before Visiting Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu tours from Cusco are on the bucket list of many travelers planning their South American adventure, and it's easy to understand why. This ancient Inca citadel perched high in the Andes is one of the world's most spectacular archaeological sites.

But visiting Machu Picchu requires planning. Here's everything you need to know.

## When to Visit

**Best months:** May to October (dry season). Skies are generally clear and the views are spectacular.

**Avoid if possible:** January and February bring heavy rainfall and frequent cloud cover. The site is still beautiful but visibility can be limited.

**Shoulder seasons:** March-April and November-December offer a good balance of manageable crowds and reasonable weather.

## How to Get There

The classic route from Cusco to Machu Picchu involves a train journey from Poroy or Ollantaytambo to Aguas Calientes (the town below Machu Picchu), followed by a bus ride up the mountain to the site.

Alternatively, the famous **Inca Trail** is a 4-day trekking route that arrives at Machu Picchu via the Sun Gate. Permits for this route sell out months in advance.

## Booking Tickets

This is critical: **Machu Picchu requires a pre-booked entrance ticket** purchased on the official government website. There is a daily visitor limit and tickets often sell out, especially during peak season.

Book your tickets at: [machupicchu.gob.pe](https://www.machupicchu.gob.pe)

## Altitude & Acclimatization

Cusco sits at 3,400m elevation. Most visitors experience some altitude sickness symptoms (headache, fatigue, nausea) in the first 24-48 hours. 

**Tips for acclimatization:**
- Arrive 2-3 days before your planned activities
- Drink plenty of coca tea
- Avoid alcohol for the first couple of days
- Take it slowly; rest when tired
- Consider altitude medication (consult your doctor)

Machu Picchu itself sits at 2,430m — actually lower than Cusco — so once you're there, the altitude is less of an issue.

## What to Combine with Machu Picchu

Most visitors to Cusco combine Machu Picchu with other attractions:

- **Sacred Valley** — the Urubamba Valley between Cusco and Machu Picchu is dotted with Inca ruins, traditional markets, and beautiful scenery
- **Rainbow Mountain** — a spectacular (though demanding) day hike to a naturally multicolored mountain
- **The Peruvian Amazon** — the most biodiverse place on Earth is just a day's drive from Cusco

## Practical Tips

- Arrive at the site as early as possible — crowds build throughout the morning
- Bring layers — mornings can be cool, afternoons warm
- Waterproof your bag — afternoon showers are common year-round
- Hire a guide — a knowledgeable guide transforms the experience
- Bring your own snacks — food inside the site is limited and expensive
