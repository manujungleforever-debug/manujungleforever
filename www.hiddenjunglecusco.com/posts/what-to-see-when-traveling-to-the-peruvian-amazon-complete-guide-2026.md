# What You Will See in the Peruvian Amazon: Complete Wildlife Guide 2026

The Amazon rainforest in Peru is one of the most biodiverse regions on Earth. The Manu National Park alone contains more bird species than the entire North American continent, and its rivers and forests teem with mammals, reptiles, and insects that exist nowhere else.

Here's your complete guide to the wildlife you can expect to encounter on a Hidden Jungle Cusco tour.

## Mammals

### Giant River Otter
The giant river otter is one of the most spectacular and endangered mammals in the Amazon. Growing up to 1.8 meters in length, these charismatic predators hunt in family groups, filling the river with their distinctive vocalizations. The oxbow lakes (cochas) in Manu are among the best places in the world to observe them.

### Jaguar
The jaguar is the apex predator of the Amazon and the holy grail for wildlife watchers. River banks at dawn and dusk are the best places for sightings — the jaguar patrols these areas in search of prey. We have documented jaguar sightings on the Madre de Dios river banks multiple times.

### Capybara
The world's largest rodent is a common and delightful sight on river banks. Families of capybaras — often numbering 10-20 individuals — spend their days grazing on riverside vegetation and cooling off in the water.

### Primates
Manu is home to 13 species of primates, including:
- **Howler monkeys** — you'll hear them before you see them, their roars carrying kilometers through the forest
- **Spider monkeys** — acrobatic and playful, often seen swinging through the canopy
- **Brown capuchins** — intelligent and curious, sometimes approaching boats on the river
- **Squirrel monkeys** — tiny and hyperactive, moving through the forest in large troops

## Birds

### Macaws
The clay licks (colpas) of Manu are famous worldwide for the spectacle of hundreds of macaws descending to eat mineral-rich clay each morning. You'll see scarlet macaws, blue-and-yellow macaws, chestnut-fronted macaws, and more — a riot of color against the green forest.

### Harpy Eagle
The harpy eagle is the world's most powerful eagle — capable of lifting prey the size of a small deer. Rare but present in Manu, a sighting of this magnificent bird is truly special.

### Hoatzin
The hoatzin is one of the Amazon's most bizarre birds — ancient in appearance, with a punk-rock crest and an unpleasant odor earned from its unusual fermenting digestive process. They are common around oxbow lakes.

## Reptiles

### Black Caiman
The black caiman can grow to over 4 meters in length and is a dominant predator of the Amazon's waterways. River safaris at dusk and dawn frequently result in sightings — their eyes reflecting red in torchlight from the riverbank.

### Anaconda
The green anaconda is the world's heaviest snake. Hidden Jungle Cusco guides are experienced at locating these magnificent creatures in the riverside vegetation where they spend much of their time.

## When to Go

The best time for wildlife viewing in Manu is during the dry season (May to October), when animals concentrate around water sources and the rivers are lower, making navigation easier. However, wildlife is present year-round and we operate tours in all months.
