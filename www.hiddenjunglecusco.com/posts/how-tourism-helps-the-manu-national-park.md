# How Tourism Helps the Manu National Park

Nestled in southeastern Peru, the Manu National Park is a remarkable UNESCO World Heritage Site. Spanning 1.5 million hectares of pristine Amazon rainforest and cloud forest, it's home to extraordinary biodiversity — including over 1,000 species of birds, 200 species of mammals, and plant species still unknown to science.

But this paradise faces real threats: illegal logging, encroachment from agriculture, and the economic pressures on local communities who depend on the land for their livelihoods.

This is where tourism — done right — becomes one of the most powerful conservation tools available.

## The Economic Argument for Conservation

The fundamental challenge of protecting wilderness areas is economic. If a family can feed their children by clearing an acre of forest for farming, they will do so. Conservation only works sustainably when the intact forest is worth more, economically, than the cleared land.

Responsible ecotourism changes this equation. When local families can earn a living as guides, lodge staff, boat operators, and suppliers to tourism businesses — all without clearing a single tree — the forest becomes valuable as forest.

## The Hidden Jungle Cusco Model

Our company employs 12 local staff members, all from Nuevo Eden and surrounding communities. We pay above-average wages and provide year-round employment, reducing the economic pressure to convert forest to farmland.

Additionally, our lodge and trails occupy approximately 400 hectares of privately-owned forest that remains intact specifically because of tourism. If we were not operating, this land would face pressure to be converted.

## Wildlife Habituates to Responsible Tourism

A counterintuitive truth of wildlife tourism: animals near regular, respectful human presence actually become easier to observe and photograph. Our boat safaris follow strict protocols — maintaining safe distances, switching off engines near sensitive wildlife, never pursuing animals — that have resulted in remarkably relaxed wildlife behavior in our core areas.

This habituation makes for better wildlife viewing AND better science: researchers who visit our area benefit from data collected over years of careful observation.

## Conservation Through Community

The most important conservation story in Manu isn't told in scientific papers — it's told in the lives of the families who live here. When those families prosper through tourism, they become the forest's most powerful advocates and protectors.
