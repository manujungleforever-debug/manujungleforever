# Five Reasons to Visit Manu National Park

Many travelers who visit Peru tick the obvious boxes: Machu Picchu, the Sacred Valley, Cusco's colonial architecture. These are all extraordinary places. But those who skip the Amazon are missing what may be Peru's greatest treasure.

Here are five compelling reasons why Manu National Park deserves a place on your itinerary.

## 1. The Most Biodiverse Place on Earth

This is not hyperbole. Manu National Park holds a legitimate claim to being the most biodiverse place on the planet.

The numbers are staggering:
- **1,000+ species of birds** — more than all of North America
- **200+ species of mammals** — including 13 primate species alone
- **5,000+ plant species** — many still unnamed by science
- **15,000+ insect species** — a fraction of the estimated total

Spending a week in Manu and not seeing something extraordinary is essentially impossible.

## 2. You'll See the Jaguar's Footprint

We don't guarantee a jaguar sighting — no one can. But we can guarantee you'll see evidence of jaguars: paw prints in the riverside sand, claw marks on trees, the silence that falls when one is near.

And occasionally — on dawn boat safaris when the river is quiet and the mist is rising — you'll see something large and spotted on the bank that makes your breath catch. It happens.

## 3. Accessible Without a Flight

Unlike the more famous Iquitos region of the Amazon (accessible only by plane from Lima), Manu is reached by road from Cusco. This two-day journey through the Andes is itself a spectacular adventure, passing through three distinct climate zones: highland puna, cloud forest, and lowland Amazon.

This road accessibility makes Manu tours from Cusco an incredibly efficient use of your travel time — no additional internal flights required.

## 4. Real Local Guides

The guides who work in Manu are mostly from the communities of Nuevo Eden, Salvación, and Boca Manu — villages that have coexisted with the jungle for generations. Their knowledge goes far beyond what any textbook can teach.

Our guides know individual trees by their smell. They can identify a monkey species by the sound of a single branch bending. They read the river like a map.

## 5. You're Supporting Conservation Just by Visiting

When you visit Manu with Hidden Jungle Cusco, your tour fees go directly into the local economy — to guide salaries, lodge staff, boat operators, and local food suppliers. This creates a direct economic incentive for local communities to protect the forest rather than convert it.

Your visit is an act of conservation.
