# Climate Change in Manu National Park

The Manu National Park is one of the most biodiverse places on our planet. Spanning 1.5 million hectares of pristine Peruvian Amazon, it is home to more bird species than all of Europe combined, and its rivers teem with life found nowhere else on Earth.

But climate change is threatening this paradise — and the threat is accelerating.

## How Climate Change Affects Manu

### Shifting Rainfall Patterns

Our guides have noticed significant changes in rainfall patterns over the past decade. The dry season is becoming longer and more severe in some years, while the rainy season brings increasingly intense flooding events. These extremes disrupt the breeding cycles of river turtles, the nesting behavior of macaws, and the migration patterns of fish.

### Temperature Rise in the Cloud Forest

The cloud forest corridor between Cusco and Manu — the remarkable transitional zone that visitors pass through on every tour — is particularly vulnerable to warming temperatures. As temperatures rise, the cloud layer rises too, effectively shrinking the cloud forest habitat from below. Species that can only survive in the cool, misty conditions of the cloud forest have nowhere to go.

### Glacial Melt Affecting River Systems

The rivers of Manu are fed partly by glacial meltwater from the Andes. As Andean glaciers continue their rapid retreat, river flow patterns are changing — with higher flows in the short term as glaciers melt faster, followed by permanently reduced flows as the glaciers disappear entirely.

## What We Are Doing

Hidden Jungle Cusco is committed to being part of the solution:

- **Carbon neutral operations** — we are working toward offsetting the carbon emissions of all our vehicles through reforestation partnerships
- **Wildlife monitoring** — our guides document climate-related changes in wildlife behavior and share data with conservation researchers
- **Community education** — we regularly host workshops for local farming families on sustainable land management practices
- **Forest protection** — every tour we operate directly funds the protection of over 400 hectares of private forest in the buffer zone
